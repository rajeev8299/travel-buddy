Varanasi sits on a crescent-shaped bend of the Ganga in eastern Uttar Pradesh. Along its western bank, a long line of stone steps — the **ghats** — runs down to the water, and behind them rise temples, palaces and the tightly packed lanes of the old city. Few places anywhere combine so much daily life, devotion and history in such a small space.

The city is known by three names — Kashi, Banaras and Varanasi — and each tells part of its story. You can read about them in [Kashi, Banaras, Varanasi: the city's names](/explore/kashi-banaras-varanasi).

## Why people come

For Hindus, Varanasi is one of the most sacred places in India. Pilgrims come to bathe in the Ganga, to visit the [Kashi Vishwanath Temple](/places/temples/kashi-vishwanath), and to perform rites for family members who have died. **Tradition** holds that dying in Kashi brings liberation (*moksha*), and some people come to the city to spend their last days here. You can read more in [Religious and spiritual significance](/explore/spiritual-significance).

Varanasi matters to other traditions too. A short drive away, [Sarnath](/places/heritage/sarnath) is where Buddhist tradition places the Buddha's first teaching, and Jain tradition associates several *tirthankaras* with the city. See [Varanasi in Buddhism and Jainism](/explore/buddhism-and-jainism).

Many travellers come simply to experience a city that feels unlike anywhere else: the first boat on the river at dawn, the evening [Ganga Aarti](/things-to-do/ganga-aarti), the music, the food and the extraordinary crafts, especially [Banarasi silk](/explore/banarasi-silk).

## The shape of the city

It helps to picture Varanasi in three parts:

- **The riverfront.** Roughly 84 ghats line the western bank, from [Assi Ghat](/places/ghats/assi) in the south to [Raj Ghat](/places/ghats/raj) in the north. [VERIFY: the commonly cited number of ghats, which varies by source — Varanasi Nagar Nigam] Some are for bathing and worship, some host ceremonies, and two — [Manikarnika](/places/ghats/manikarnika) and [Harishchandra](/places/ghats/harishchandra) — are cremation ghats.
- **The old city.** Behind the central ghats lies a maze of narrow lanes (*galis*) full of shrines, shops, homes and food stalls. Cars can't enter most of it, so you walk.
- **The wider city.** South of the old city are [Banaras Hindu University](/explore/banaras-hindu-university) and temples such as [Durga Kund](/places/temples/durga-kund) and [Sankat Mochan](/places/temples/sankat-mochan). Across the river is [Ramnagar Fort](/places/heritage/ramnagar-fort), and to the north-east lies Sarnath.

The opposite (eastern) bank of the river is largely open sandbank, which is why the view across from the ghats feels so wide and empty.

## A city of many layers

Varanasi's past is long and layered. **Archaeological evidence** from excavations on the Rajghat plateau points to very early settlement, and the kingdom of Kashi appears in early Buddhist texts. The temples and ghats you see today, however, are mostly much more recent: many were built or rebuilt in the 18th and 19th centuries by patrons from across India. Our [history of Varanasi](/explore/history) explains how these layers fit together, separating documented history and archaeology from tradition and belief.

## How to experience Varanasi well

- **Get up early at least once.** The riverfront is at its most alive just after first light. A [sunrise boat ride](/things-to-do/sunrise-boat-ride) or a [morning walk along the ghats](/things-to-do/morning-ghat-walk) is the best introduction.
- **Walk the lanes, slowly.** The old city rewards wandering, but it is easy to get lost — a local guide helps the first time.
- **Be respectful at sacred sites.** Dress modestly, remove shoes where asked and follow photography rules. At the cremation ghats, never photograph cremations or mourners. See [Responsible and respectful travel](/travel-guide/responsible-travel).
- **Plan around the season.** Winter (roughly October to March) is the most comfortable time to visit. Summers are very hot, and during the monsoon the river can rise high enough to close the boats. See [Best time to visit](/travel-guide/best-time-to-visit).
- **Check practical details before you go.** Temple timings, entry rules and boat operations change, especially on festival days. Our pages show when each practical detail was last verified; anything marked "being verified" hasn't been confirmed yet.
