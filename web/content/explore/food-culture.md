Food in Varanasi is shaped by the city's religious life, its seasons and its long history as a crossroads. Much of the old city's food is vegetarian, and many dishes are tied to particular times of day or year.

## A day of eating

- **Breakfast** in Banaras often means **[kachori-sabzi](/food/kachori-sabzi)** — fried kachori with spiced potato curry — followed by hot **jalebi**.
- **Through the day**, chaat stalls serve dishes such as **[tamatar chaat](/food/tamatar-chaat)**, and lassi is served in clay cups (*kulhads*).
- **After a meal**, many locals end with a **[Banarasi paan](/food/banarasi-paan)**.

## Food and the seasons

Some dishes appear only at certain times of year. **[Malaiyo](/food/malaiyo)**, a delicate milk froth flavoured with saffron and cardamom, is a winter speciality, and **chooda matar** (flattened rice with green peas) appears when fresh peas are in season. ## Food and faith

Temple offerings (*prasad*) are an important part of food culture — the laddoos offered at [Sankat Mochan](/places/temples/sankat-mochan) are a well-known example. Many households follow vegetarian diets, and some avoid onion and garlic on religious grounds.

## Eating well and safely

Choose busy stalls where food is cooked fresh in front of you, and drink bottled or filtered water. See [Drinking water and food safety](/travel-guide/food-and-water-safety) and our [food walk guide](/things-to-do/food-walks).
