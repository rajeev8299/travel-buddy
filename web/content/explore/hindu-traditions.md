Varanasi holds a special place in Hindu tradition, and much of what you see in the city — the temples, the riverside rituals, the pilgrims walking barefoot through the lanes — follows from it. This short guide introduces the main ideas; all of them are matters of **tradition and belief** rather than historical record.

## Shiva's city

**Tradition:** Kashi is regarded as the eternal home of Shiva. The [Kashi Vishwanath Temple](/places/temples/kashi-vishwanath) is its centre, and devotees count it among the twelve *jyotirlingas*. The fierce form of Shiva worshipped at [Kaal Bhairav](/places/temples/kaal-bhairav) is believed to guard the city.

## The sacred zone

Kashi is understood as a sacred territory with boundaries marked by rivers and shrines. **Tradition** describes it as resting on Shiva's trident, beyond the reach of the cycles of destruction. Pilgrims walk circuits around this zone — most famously the **Panchakroshi Yatra** — stopping at many shrines on the way. [VERIFY: route details — pilgrimage authorities]

## Rites of life and death

Varanasi is a place for rites at every stage of life: sacred-thread ceremonies, weddings, prayers for health and, above all, rites for the dead. **Tradition** holds that dying in Kashi brings liberation (*moksha*), which is why cremations take place at [Manikarnika](/places/ghats/manikarnika) and [Harishchandra](/places/ghats/harishchandra) ghats, and why families come to perform rites for their ancestors.

## Many deities

Although Shiva is central, Varanasi has temples to Vishnu, the goddess in many forms, Hanuman, Ganesha and countless local deities. See [Places to Visit: temples](/places/temples) and [Religious and spiritual significance](/explore/spiritual-significance).
