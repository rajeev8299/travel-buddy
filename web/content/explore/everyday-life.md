Beyond the temples and ceremonies, Varanasi is a living, working city. Much of its charm lies in ordinary moments, and the best way to see them is simply to slow down and watch.

## Morning on the ghats

Before sunrise, the ghats fill with people: bathers, priests setting up for the day, people doing yoga, and old friends meeting for a chat. Boat families prepare for the day's first passengers. By mid-morning, washermen spread laundry on the steps at certain ghats, and children head to school.

## Chai and conversation

Small chai stalls are the city's meeting places. Locals linger over clay cups of sweet, milky tea, discussing news, cricket and politics. Joining them — politely — is one of the easiest ways to feel part of the city.

## The lanes

In the old city's lanes, life happens at close quarters: shopkeepers open their shutters, sweet-makers fry jalebis, and cows wander between motorbikes. Shrines in doorways receive flowers and prayers from passers-by.

## Evening

As the light fades, the riverfront fills again for the evening Aarti, music drifts from homes and temples, and families stroll along the steps.

## Being a good guest
