# Kashi, Banaras, Varanasi: one city, three names

Ask a boatman, a priest and a railway clerk what this city is called, and you may get three different answers. **Kashi**, **Banaras** and **Varanasi** are all names for the same place. They are used side by side every day, and each has its own history and feeling. Understanding them is a good first step to understanding the city.

## At a glance

| Name | Language and meaning | Where you'll hear it |
|---|---|---|
| **Kashi** | Sanskrit; usually explained as "the luminous" or "city of light" | Religious and devotional contexts; "Kashi Vishwanath" |
| **Varanasi** | Sanskrit; commonly linked to the Varuna and Assi rivers | The official name of the city and district |
| **Banaras** (Benares) | Popular form that developed from "Varanasi" | Everyday speech, silk ("Banarasi sari"), music, food, BHU |

## Kashi: the city of light

**What the name means.** *Kashi* is most often explained as coming from the Sanskrit root *kash*, "to shine". It is commonly translated as "the luminous one" or "the city of light". [VERIFY: etymology, against a scholarly source]

**Documented history.** Kashi is also the name of an ancient **kingdom** (*Kashi janapada*) named in early Indian literature, including Buddhist texts, which describe it as one of the major kingdoms of the 6th century BCE. [VERIFY: the list of the "sixteen mahajanapadas" and Kashi's inclusion]

**Tradition & belief.** In Hindu tradition, Kashi is the city of Shiva, which he is said never to leave. Texts such as the *Kashi Khanda* praise it as a place where the divine light is especially present, and where death brings liberation. Devotees often call the whole sacred zone of the city simply "Kashi".

## Varanasi: between two rivers

**What the name means.** *Varanasi* is widely explained as the city lying between two streams that flow into the Ganga: the **Varuna** to the north and the **Assi** to the south. The historic riverfront does indeed lie between these two points. [VERIFY: the derivation, against a scholarly source; some scholars also link the name to the Varuna alone]

**Documented history.** The name appears in ancient literature, including Buddhist texts such as the Jataka tales. After Indian independence, **Varanasi** was adopted as the city's official name in 1956. [VERIFY: date and instrument of the official renaming]

## Banaras: the name of everyday life

**What the name means.** *Banaras* (written *Benares* in British-era English) is a popular form of *Varanasi*, developed through everyday speech over centuries.

**Where you'll hear it.** Banaras is the name of the city's living culture: **Banarasi silk saris**, **Banarasi paan**, the **Banaras gharana** of music, and **Banaras Hindu University**. Many residents simply call themselves *Banarasi*.

## Why three names still matter

The three names reflect different sides of the same place:
- **Kashi** is the sacred city: Shiva's city and the city of liberation.
- **Varanasi** is the historical and official city, set between its rivers.
- **Banaras** is the everyday city of silk, music, food, wit and its famous unhurried pace of life (*masti*).

You'll find all three in signs, conversations and this website. We use **Varanasi** as our standard name, **Kashi** where religious tradition is the subject, and **Banaras** when we talk about the city's crafts, food and culture.

## Other names you may meet

- **Avimukta**, "never forsaken", a name used in religious literature for the sacred zone that, according to tradition, Shiva never abandons.
- **Anandavana**, "forest of bliss", and **Mahashmashana**, "great cremation ground", are other traditional epithets. [VERIFY: epithets, against a scholarly source]

## Frequently asked questions

**Are Kashi, Banaras and Varanasi the same place?**
Yes. They are three names for the same city, used side by side.

**What is the official name?**
Varanasi. It has been the official name of the city and district since 1956. [VERIFY]

**Why is it called the "City of Light"?**
"Kashi" is commonly explained as coming from a Sanskrit root meaning "to shine". In Hindu tradition the city is associated with the light of Shiva.

**Is "Benares" still used?**
It is the older English spelling of Banaras. You'll still see it in historical names and some institutions.
