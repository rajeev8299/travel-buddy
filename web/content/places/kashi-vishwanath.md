# Shri Kashi Vishwanath Temple

The Shri Kashi Vishwanath Temple is the spiritual centre of Varanasi. It is dedicated to Shiva as Vishwanath, "Lord of the Universe", and is one of the most visited Hindu temples in India. For many pilgrims, a visit to Varanasi is not complete without darshan here. For other visitors it is the best introduction to the faith and daily life of the old city.

This guide explains what you will see, how the visit works and how to prepare. It also covers how to be respectful while you are there.

## Introduction and significance

Kashi Vishwanath stands in the heart of the old city, a short walk from the Ganga. Since the Kashi Vishwanath Dham corridor opened, the temple has been connected to the riverfront by a wide pedestrian complex. For centuries before that, the only way in was through narrow lanes.

**Tradition & belief.** Devotees regard the temple's Shiva linga as one of the twelve **Jyotirlingas**, the most sacred shrines of Shiva in Hindu tradition. According to tradition, Kashi is Shiva's own city and is never abandoned by him. Many Hindus believe that dying in Kashi brings liberation (*moksha*). This belief shapes much of the city's religious life, from its temples to its cremation ghats.

## History

### Documented history
- Temples to Vishwanath have stood in Varanasi for many centuries. Historical sources record earlier temples on or near the site being destroyed and rebuilt several times. One such destruction took place in 1669, during the reign of the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb.
- **The present temple was built in 1780** under Ahilyabai Holkar, the Maratha queen of Indore, who is remembered for building and restoring temples and ghats across India.
- In the 19th century, Maharaja Ranjit Singh of Punjab donated gold that was used to plate the temple's spires. This is why it is often called the "Golden Temple" of Varanasi. [VERIFY: exact year of the gold donation, against a published source]
- The temple is managed by the **Shri Kashi Vishwanath Temple Trust**, constituted under Uttar Pradesh state legislation. [VERIFY: name and year of the governing Act]
- **The Kashi Vishwanath Dham corridor was inaugurated in December 2021.** It opened up the area around the temple and created a direct walkway down to the Ganga.

### A note on the Gyanvapi complex
The **Gyanvapi mosque** stands next to the temple complex. Questions about the history of the site are the subject of **ongoing legal proceedings**, and we do not take a position on them. Visitors should be aware that security in this area is tight, and that the site is significant to more than one community.

### Tradition & belief
- According to Hindu tradition, the Vishwanath linga appeared as a column of light (*jyoti*) that revealed Shiva's supremacy. That is the meaning behind the term "Jyotirlinga".
- Devotees traditionally pair a visit to Vishwanath with darshan at the **Annapurna Temple** nearby, honouring the goddess who is believed to nourish the city. Many also visit **Kaal Bhairav**, regarded as the guardian (*kotwal*) of Kashi.

## Architecture and what to notice

- **The gilded spires (*shikhara*)**, plated with gold, are the temple's best-known feature. You can see them from the corridor.
- **The sanctum is small** and is reached through a crowded, fast-moving darshan line. Most people get only a brief moment before the linga. Plan for that and don't expect to linger.
- **The corridor** brings together restored shrines, courtyards and facilities. It leads down to the river at the ghats. Notice how it contrasts with the old lanes: the complex itself is modern, but many of the small shrines inside it are old and were restored during the project.
- **Around the temple**, the surrounding lanes (*galis*) are lined with shops selling flowers, *prasad*, brass and religious items. This is part of the experience, but it is busy.

## Rules: what to carry and what to avoid

Rules are set by the Temple Trust and can change. The guidance below is general. Always follow the current official notice and the instructions of security staff.

- **Travel light.** Expect thorough security checks. Leave valuables, large bags and anything you don't need at your hotel.
- **Phones and electronics:** it is widely reported that these are not allowed inside the sanctum area. [VERIFY: current rule and locker availability]
- **Dress modestly.** Cover your shoulders and knees. Footwear is removed before entering the shrine areas.
- **Offerings.** Flowers, *bel* leaves and milk are traditional offerings, sold in the lanes nearby. Check current rules on what may be offered at the sanctum. [VERIFY]
- **Touts.** You may be approached by people offering "fast" or "VIP" darshan. Use only official arrangements announced by the Temple Trust.

## Photography etiquette

- Photography inside the temple and sanctum is generally not permitted. [VERIFY: current rule]
- Photography is usually possible in parts of the corridor. Follow the signs and the instructions of staff.
- Never photograph people at prayer without their permission, and don't use flash near shrines.

## Accessibility and senior-friendly guidance

- **Approaching through the corridor from the river side** involves fewer of the narrow lanes, but it still means walking, steps and long queues. [VERIFY: current step-free routes, ramps, wheelchair provision and any assistance for elderly or differently-abled visitors — Temple Trust]
- **Choose a quieter time.** Early morning or late evening is usually calmer than midday, and festival days are best avoided if mobility is limited. (Suggested)
- **Rest and water.** Carry water and plan a rest stop after darshan. The crowd and heat can be tiring, especially in summer.
- **Ask for help.** Senior travellers can request a local companion from our assistance service (`/services/assistance/senior-friendly`) to help with queues and walking.

## Getting there and last-mile walking

Kashi Vishwanath is in the **old city, where vehicles cannot go**. Every visit ends with a walk. How long that walk is depends on where you start and on current traffic restrictions.

- **By road:** cars, autos and e-rickshaws usually drop visitors at the edge of the old city. The common drop-off is around the **Godowlia–Maidagin** area, though restrictions change, especially on busy days and festivals. From there, you continue on foot through the lanes or along the corridor approach. [VERIFY: current vehicle drop-off points and gate names — Varanasi Police traffic advisories]
- **By river:** you can reach the corridor from the ghats. Many visitors combine darshan with a boat ride or a walk along the riverfront.
- **Expect** crowds, uneven surfaces, steps, two-wheelers in the lanes, and cows and shops spilling into narrow paths. Wear comfortable footwear that is easy to take off.

See **Last-mile walking** (`/travel-guide/last-mile-walking`) for current drop-off guidance.

## Suggested visit duration

**Suggested: 1.5 to 3 hours**, including security, darshan and some time in the corridor. Allow much longer on Mondays, during Sawan and at festivals. Pair the visit with the Annapurna Temple and an evening at Dashashwamedh for a half-day plan.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Kashi Vishwanath one of the twelve Jyotirlingas?**
Yes. In Hindu tradition it is counted among the twelve Jyotirlingas, the most sacred shrines of Shiva.

**Can non-Hindus visit?**
[VERIFY: current Temple Trust policy on entry for visitors of other faiths and foreign nationals.] Anyone can walk through the public parts of the corridor. Please dress modestly and follow staff instructions.

**Can I take my phone inside?**
It is widely reported that phones are not allowed beyond the security points, and that lockers are provided. [VERIFY: current rule]

**Is there a paid fast-track darshan?**
Check the official Temple Trust website for any current paid or special darshan options. Don't buy "VIP darshan" from people in the lanes.

**When is the temple least crowded?**
Usually early morning and late evening on weekdays other than Monday. It is always busiest on festival days and during Sawan. (Suggested)

**How do I reach the temple if I can't walk far?**
Plan the shortest current approach with our team or a local companion. Avoid peak days, and ask about assistance at the official facilities. [VERIFY: current assistance provisions]

**What is the Kashi Vishwanath Dham corridor?**
A redevelopment of the temple surroundings, inaugurated in December 2021, that links the temple to the Ganga with a wide pedestrian complex.

**Can I offer milk or flowers?**
These are traditional offerings and are sold nearby. Follow the current Temple Trust rules on what may be offered at the sanctum.
