For millions of people, Varanasi is not just a place to visit but a place of profound religious meaning. Understanding why helps you make sense of what you see on the ghats and in the temples, and how to behave respectfully while you're there.

This page describes what devotees believe and the practices that follow from those beliefs. Beliefs are labelled as **tradition** — they are matters of faith rather than historical fact.

## Kashi, the city of Shiva

**Tradition:** in Hindu belief, Kashi is Shiva's own city, chosen by him as his home. The [Kashi Vishwanath Temple](/places/temples/kashi-vishwanath), dedicated to Shiva as "Lord of the Universe", is its spiritual heart, and many devotees regard it as one of the twelve *jyotirlingas* — shrines where Shiva is believed to have manifested as a column of light.

Shiva's presence is felt across the city: countless *lingas* stand in temples, in wall niches and at the foot of trees, and the greeting "Har Har Mahadev" (an invocation of Shiva) is heard everywhere.

## A tirtha: a crossing place

The word *tirtha* means a ford or crossing place. **Tradition:** a tirtha is a place where it is easier to cross from the everyday world to the divine. Kashi is regarded as one of the greatest tirthas, and the Ganga as the most sacred of rivers.

That is why so much happens at the water's edge. Pilgrims bathe in the river at dawn, offer flowers and lamps, and perform rituals on the ghats. See [The Ganga and the riverfront](/explore/the-ganga).

## Liberation in Kashi

**Tradition:** one of the most important beliefs about Varanasi is that a person who dies here is freed from the cycle of rebirth and attains *moksha* (liberation). Many devotees believe that Shiva himself whispers the words of liberation into the ear of the dying.

Because of this belief:

- Families bring the bodies of their relatives to be cremated at [Manikarnika](/places/ghats/manikarnika) and [Harishchandra](/places/ghats/harishchandra) ghats.
- Some elderly people come to Varanasi to spend their last days, staying in special guesthouses.
- Families come to perform rites for ancestors.

For visitors, this is the most important thing to understand about the cremation ghats: they are places of grief and sacred duty for real families, not tourist sights. Keep your distance, stay quiet, and **never photograph cremations or mourners**.

## The sacred zone and pilgrimage routes

**Tradition:** Kashi is understood as a sacred territory, not just a city. Pilgrims walk circuits that define its boundaries. The best known is the **Panchakroshi Yatra**, a long walking pilgrimage around the sacred zone with stops at many shrines along the way. ## Temples and deities

Although Shiva is central, Varanasi has temples to many deities: the goddess at [Durga Kund](/places/temples/durga-kund) and [Annapurna](/places/temples/annapurna), Hanuman at [Sankat Mochan](/places/temples/sankat-mochan), the city's guardian [Kaal Bhairav](/places/temples/kaal-bhairav) and many more. Visiting several temples in sequence is a common way for pilgrims to structure their stay — see [temple pilgrimage circuits](/things-to-do/temple-circuits).

## The daily rhythm of devotion

Devotion in Varanasi follows the day:

- **Before dawn and at sunrise:** bathing in the Ganga, prayers and offerings on the ghats; early temple rituals.
- **Through the day:** temple visits, rituals for families, readings and teaching.
- **At dusk:** the evening **Ganga Aarti**, an offering of light to the river, performed with lamps, bells and chants at [Dashashwamedh Ghat](/places/ghats/dashashwamedh) and other ghats. See [Watching the Ganga Aarti](/things-to-do/ganga-aarti).

## More than one tradition

Varanasi is sacred to Buddhists and Jains as well as Hindus — see [Varanasi in Buddhism and Jainism](/explore/buddhism-and-jainism). The city has long-established Muslim communities, whose weavers are central to [Banarasi silk](/explore/banarasi-silk), as well as Sikh and Christian places of worship. Kabir, the 15th-century poet-saint whose verses are loved across religious boundaries, is closely associated with the city. Varanasi's spiritual life is plural, and respectful visitors are welcome across it.

## Being a respectful visitor

- Dress modestly — shoulders and knees covered — when visiting temples and ghats.
- Remove your shoes where others do, and follow the rules posted at each temple.
- Ask before photographing people, especially during rituals.
- Don't touch shrines, offerings or people performing rituals.
- At cremation ghats: no photography, no crowding, no loud conversation.
