Everything in Varanasi faces the river. The Ganga (Ganges) is the reason the city is here, the setting for its rituals and the backdrop to daily life. This page explains what the river means, how it shapes the city through the year, and how to treat it with respect.

## A river that flows north

At Varanasi, the Ganga makes a long, gentle curve and, for a stretch, flows towards the north. The city's ghats line the outer, western side of this crescent, which gives the riverfront its famous sweeping view.

**Tradition:** a north-flowing (*uttaravahini*) stretch of the Ganga is regarded as especially auspicious, because the river is seen as turning back towards its Himalayan source. Devotees count this among the reasons Varanasi is so sacred.

The eastern bank, opposite the ghats, is mostly open sandbank and floodplain. That's why the view from the city across the water feels so wide and open, and why the sunrise over the far bank is such a striking sight.

## The sacred river

**Tradition:** in Hindu belief, the Ganga is a goddess, Ganga Ma (Mother Ganga), who descended from heaven to earth. Bathing in her waters is believed to wash away sins, and her water is collected and carried home by pilgrims for use in rituals.

On the ghats you will see:

- **Bathing** at dawn, often accompanied by prayers and offerings to the rising sun.
- **Offerings** of flowers and small lamps (*diyas*) floated on the water.
- **Rituals** for family events and for ancestors, guided by priests on the steps.
- The evening **Ganga Aarti**, an offering of light to the river, performed with lamps, bells and chants. See [Watching the Ganga Aarti](/things-to-do/ganga-aarti).
- **Cremations** at [Manikarnika](/places/ghats/manikarnika) and [Harishchandra](/places/ghats/harishchandra) ghats. Please keep your distance and never photograph them.

## The river through the seasons

The Ganga changes dramatically through the year, and this affects what you can do:

- **Winter (roughly October to March):** the river is lower and calm, mornings are misty and cool, and boats run regularly. This is the most popular time to visit.
- **Summer (roughly April to June):** the water is at its lowest and the weather very hot. Early mornings and evenings are the best times on the river.
- **Monsoon (roughly July to September):** heavy rains in the catchment raise the river, sometimes high enough to flood the lower steps and reach the buildings above. ## Boats on the Ganga

Seeing the ghats from the water is one of the essential Varanasi experiences. You can choose a hand-rowed boat or a motorboat, a shared or a private boat, at sunrise or sunset. ## Life and work on the river

The Ganga is also a working river. Boat families have worked here for generations, *dhobis* (washermen) wash clothes at certain ghats, and fishermen still cast nets in the early morning. Children swim and play cricket on the steps in the dry season. Watching this everyday life is as much a part of Varanasi as the temples.

## The river's health

The Ganga faces serious pollution from sewage, industry and the sheer number of people who depend on it. Governments have run clean-up programmes for decades, including the national **Namami Gange** programme launched in 2014. [VERIFY: programme dates and current status — National Mission for Clean Ganga] Water quality varies along the river and through the year.

For visitors, this means:

- Think carefully before bathing, and avoid swallowing river water.
- Don't add to the problem: no plastic, no litter, and use biodegradable offerings.
- Support efforts by local groups that clean the ghats.

The **Gangetic river dolphin**, India's national aquatic animal, still lives in parts of the Ganga, and sightings are occasionally reported near Varanasi. [VERIFY: recent sighting reports near Varanasi — published wildlife sources]

## Respecting the river

- Keep the river and ghats clean.
- Dress modestly on the ghats, and especially if you choose to bathe.
- Ask before photographing people bathing or praying.
- Never photograph cremations.
- Follow boat safety rules and any notices from the administration.
