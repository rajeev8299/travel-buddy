# Sarnath: where the Buddha first taught

Sarnath lies a short drive north-east of Varanasi. [VERIFY: distance from central Varanasi, from an official source] It is one of the most important places in the Buddhist world. According to Buddhist tradition, the Buddha gave his **first sermon** here after attaining enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, "setting the wheel of the Dharma in motion". Today Sarnath is calm and green. Ancient brick ruins, a great stupa, an outstanding museum and temples built by Buddhist communities from across Asia make it the perfect counterpoint to the intensity of the ghats.

## Introduction and significance

**Tradition & belief.** Buddhists revere Sarnath, known in early texts as **Isipatana** and the **Deer Park** (*Mrigadava*), as the place where the Buddha taught his first five disciples. The sermon, known as the *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta*, sets out the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path. Sarnath is one of the four principal Buddhist pilgrimage sites associated with the Buddha's life, alongside Lumbini, Bodh Gaya and Kushinagar.

Sarnath is also significant in **Jain tradition**. It is associated with **Shreyansanatha**, the eleventh Tirthankara, and a Jain temple stands near the Dhamek Stupa. [VERIFY: the specific tradition associating Shreyansanatha with Sarnath, and the temple's name]

## History

### Documented history
- The Mauryan emperor **Ashoka** (3rd century BCE) built monuments at Sarnath, including a stone **pillar** with an inscription. Sarnath grew into a major monastic centre over the following centuries.
- The Chinese pilgrim **Xuanzang** visited in the 7th century CE and described its monasteries. [VERIFY: details of his account]
- The site later declined. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, surveys and excavations, including work associated with **Alexander Cunningham** and later the **Archaeological Survey of India**, uncovered its monuments and sculpture.
- **Sarnath Museum** was set up in **1910** to house the finds. It is one of the oldest site museums of the ASI. [VERIFY: "oldest site museum" claim]

### Archaeological evidence
- **The Lion Capital of Ashoka** was found during the **1904–05 excavations** at Sarnath. It shows four lions standing back to back. It was **adopted as the State Emblem of India** in 1950, and the wheel (*chakra*) on its base appears at the centre of India's national flag.
- **The Ashokan pillar**: the broken shaft of the pillar on which the capital originally stood remains on site.
- **The Dhamek Stupa** in its present form is largely a **Gupta-period** structure (around the 5th–6th centuries CE), built over earlier foundations.
- **The remains of monasteries and the Dharmarajika Stupa** (attributed to Ashoka) are visible as brick foundations across the site. [VERIFY: the account of the Dharmarajika Stupa being dismantled for building material in the late 18th century]

## Architecture and what to notice

### Dhamek Stupa
The great cylindrical stupa that dominates the site. Look for the bands of **finely carved floral and geometric patterns** in stone around its lower part. Many pilgrims walk around it clockwise (*pradakshina*) in silent prayer, and you may see monks and pilgrims from many countries chanting here. (`/places/heritage/dhamek-stupa`)

### Chaukhandi Stupa
A short distance from the main site stands a tall brick mound with an **octagonal tower** on top. **Tradition & belief:** Buddhist tradition holds that this is where the Buddha met his five former companions on his way to Sarnath. **Documented history:** the octagonal tower was added in **1588**, during Akbar's reign, to commemorate a visit by his father, the Mughal emperor **Humayun**. An inscription records this. (`/places/heritage/chaukhandi-stupa`)

### Mulagandha Kuti Vihara
A modern temple built by the **Maha Bodhi Society** and opened in **1931**. Inside, the walls are covered with **murals of the Buddha's life by the Japanese painter Kosetsu Nosu**. Beside the temple is a Bodhi tree, which tradition holds was grown from a cutting of the sacred tree in Sri Lanka. [VERIFY: provenance of the Bodhi tree] (`/places/heritage/mulagandha-kuti-vihara`)

### Sarnath Museum and the Lion Capital
The museum's highlight is the **Lion Capital**: polished sandstone, with four lions above a base carved with animals and wheels. It also holds some of the finest early Buddhist sculpture in India, including a celebrated seated **"teaching Buddha"** of the Gupta period. (`/places/heritage/sarnath-museum`)

### Temples of the Buddhist world
Around the site are temples and monasteries built by Buddhist communities and countries, including **Tibetan, Thai, Japanese, Chinese, Sri Lankan and Myanmar** traditions. Each is in its own architectural style, and each is a quiet place to visit. [VERIFY: current list of temples open to visitors]

## Rules: what to carry and what to avoid

- **Dress modestly**, and remove your shoes where signs ask you to, especially in temples and monasteries.
- **Walk around stupas clockwise**, and don't climb on any ruins or monuments.
- **Carry water, a hat and sunscreen.** The site is open and can be hot.
- **Keep a respectful silence** near monks and pilgrims at prayer.
- **Bags in the museum** may need to be deposited. [VERIFY: current cloakroom rule]

## Photography etiquette

- Photographing the ruins and stupas outdoors is generally welcome. [VERIFY: any ASI restrictions, e.g. on tripods or drones]

## Accessibility and senior-friendly guidance

- The archaeological park is **largely flat**, with paths across lawns. Some areas have uneven ground, low steps and gravel. [VERIFY: wheelchair access to the site and museum]
- Distances between the main site, the museum, Chaukhandi Stupa and the temples are walkable but add up. Plan short hops by vehicle if walking is difficult. (Suggested)
- There are shaded spots, but plan rest breaks, especially from April to June.

**Suggested: half a day, about 3 to 4 hours including travel**, covering the archaeological park, the museum (if it's open that day), Chaukhandi Stupa and one or two temples. Check the museum's weekly closing day before you go.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is Sarnath important?**
According to Buddhist tradition, the Buddha gave his first sermon here. It is one of the four main Buddhist pilgrimage sites connected with his life.

**Where is the Ashoka Lion Capital?**
In the **Sarnath Museum**. It was found during the 1904–05 excavations at Sarnath and is the source of India's State Emblem.

**Is Sarnath Museum open every day?**
No. It has a weekly closing day. Check current ASI timings before you plan. [VERIFY]

**How much time do I need in Sarnath?**
Half a day is comfortable for most visitors. Allow longer if you want to visit several temples or spend time meditating.

**Can I combine Sarnath with sightseeing in Varanasi on the same day?**
Yes. A popular plan is Sarnath in the morning or early afternoon, then back to the ghats for the evening Aarti.

**Is Sarnath important to Jains too?**
Yes. Jain tradition associates Sarnath with the eleventh Tirthankara, and there is a Jain temple near the Dhamek Stupa.
