# Dashashwamedh Ghat and the Evening Ganga Aarti

Dashashwamedh is the busiest and most central of Varanasi's ghats. By day it is crowded with pilgrims, priests under bamboo umbrellas, flower sellers and boatmen. Every evening it hosts the **Ganga Aarti**, a ceremony of fire, bells and chanting offered to the river, which draws large crowds of pilgrims and travellers. If you have only one evening in Varanasi, this is where most people spend it.

## Introduction and significance

The ghat sits at the end of the main road from **Godowlia**, which makes it the most accessible point on the riverfront for most visitors. It is close to the Kashi Vishwanath corridor and to many smaller ghats on either side. It is a good place to start a riverside walk or a boat ride.

**Tradition & belief.** According to tradition, the name *Dasha-ashwamedha* ("ten horse sacrifices") comes from a legend that the god Brahma performed ten *ashwamedha* sacrifices here. Devotees consider bathing here especially meritorious.

### Tradition & belief
- The Aarti is an act of worship. Lamps are offered to the Ganga, who is revered as a goddess (*Ganga Maa*) and believed by devotees to purify sins.
- Floating a small leaf-cup lamp (*diya*) with flowers on the river is a common personal offering. Devotees make a wish or offer a prayer as they do.

## Architecture and what to notice

- **The broad stone steps** descend to the river and are crowded with umbrellas, platforms and small shrines.
- **The Aarti platforms** are raised wooden or stone platforms where priests, usually young men in matching robes, perform synchronised rituals with multi-tiered brass lamps, incense, conch shells, yak-tail whisks and flowers.
- **The river view** at dusk shows boats massing in front of the ghat to watch the Aarti from the water.
- **In the monsoon**, when the Ganga rises, the lower steps disappear underwater. The ceremony is then usually held higher up or on rooftops. [VERIFY: current flood-season arrangements for the season of visit]

## Rules: what to carry and what to avoid

- **Arrive early** if you want a place on the steps. Spots fill well before the ceremony begins. (Suggested)
- **Keep valuables close.** The crowd is dense, especially on festival days.
- **Carry little:** a small bag, water, and a shawl in winter.
- **Respect the ceremony.** Stay seated or still during the Aarti, keep voices low, and don't climb onto the platforms.
- **Diyas and flowers** are sold on the ghat. If you float one, buy it from a seller on the steps rather than from someone who approaches you with a high price.
- **Agree the boat price before boarding** for Aarti viewing, including how long you will stay.

## Photography etiquette

- Photographing the Aarti from the steps or a boat is common. Please don't use flash, block other people's view or stand up in a boat during the ceremony.
- Ask before photographing individuals, including priests, sadhus and pilgrims bathing. Some people may ask for money in return for a photo. It's fine to politely decline.

## Accessibility and senior-friendly guidance

- The ghat is a **staircase down to the river**: steep in places, often wet near the water, and crowded in the evening.
- **Watching from a boat** avoids standing in the crowd, but boarding means stepping down onto a moving boat. Ask the boatman for a hand. (Suggested)
- For wheelchair users, there is no step-free access to the lower ghat. Some viewpoints from the upper level may be possible. [VERIFY: any accessible viewing arrangements]
- If you are travelling with elders, consider a weekday outside festival periods, and agree a meeting point in case you get separated.

## Getting there and last-mile walking

- **By road:** autos and e-rickshaws usually go as far as the **Godowlia** crossing. From there it is a walk down the busy main road to the ghat. During festivals, roads may be closed to vehicles further out. [VERIFY: current drop-off points and festival restrictions — Varanasi Police]
- **Along the river:** Dashashwamedh is in the middle of the ghat chain, so you can walk to it along the riverfront from neighbouring ghats when the water is low.
- **By boat:** many visitors come by boat from Assi or other ghats and watch from the water.

## Suggested visit duration

**Suggested: 1.5 to 2.5 hours in the evening.** That includes arriving early for a place, the Aarti itself and walking out with the crowd. In the daytime, 30 to 60 minutes is enough to walk and watch ghat life.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the Ganga Aarti free?**
Watching from the ghat is free. Boat viewing is paid to the boat operator. Agree the price before boarding.

**What time does the Ganga Aarti start?**
Around sunset, so the time changes with the season. Check the current time on our **Today in Varanasi** page or with your local partner. [VERIFY: current time]

**Is it better to watch from the steps or from a boat?**
The steps put you close to the priests and the sound. A boat gives the full view of the lamps over the river and avoids the crowd on land. Both are worthwhile.

**Does the Aarti happen during the monsoon?**
According to the organisers' practice, it continues but usually moves to higher ground when the river is in flood. Boat viewing may be suspended. [VERIFY: current season's arrangements]

**Is Dashashwamedh safe at night?**
It is busy and well attended, but crowded places attract pickpockets. Keep valuables close and plan your route back in advance, especially on festival nights.

**Which is better, the Aarti at Dashashwamedh or at Assi?**
Dashashwamedh's evening Aarti is larger and busier. Assi is known for a calmer morning programme. Many visitors see both.
