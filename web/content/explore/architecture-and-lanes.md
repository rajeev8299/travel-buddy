Behind the ghats lies one of the most intricate old cities in India. Its architecture tells the story of patrons from across the country, and its narrow lanes — the **galis** — are where the city's daily life happens.

## Along the river

The riverfront is lined with palaces, temples and residences built mostly in the 18th and 19th centuries by rulers and noble families from across India, often in their own regional styles. Many stand on high plinths to protect them from the monsoon floods. Notable examples include **Man Mahal**, with its rooftop observatory ([Jantar Mantar](/places/heritage/jantar-mantar)), and the fort-like building at [Chet Singh Ghat](/places/ghats/chet-singh).

## The galis

The lanes of the old city are often only wide enough for people, bicycles and the occasional motorbike — and sometimes a cow. They wind between tall houses, opening suddenly onto tiny squares and shrines built into walls. Many lanes are named after the trades once practised there.

Getting lost is part of the experience, but it helps to:

- Go with a local guide the first time — see [heritage walking tours](/things-to-do/heritage-walks).
- Note a landmark ghat or temple to navigate back to.
- Keep to the side to let motorbikes pass, and watch your step.

## Temples big and small

Temple architecture ranges from tall *nagara*-style spires, like the deep-red [Durga Kund](/places/temples/durga-kund), to tiny shrines tucked into doorways.
