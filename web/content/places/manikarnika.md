# Manikarnika Ghat: a respectful visitor's guide

Manikarnika is the principal **cremation ghat** of Varanasi. Here, families bring their loved ones for the last rites, often from far across India. It is one of the most sacred places in the city. It is also, above all, a place of **grief and private ritual**.

This page explains what Manikarnika means, so that you can understand the city. If you visit, it shows you how to do so with the care it deserves. It is not a sight to be "seen".

## Introduction and significance

**Tradition & belief.** For many Hindus, Kashi is the city where Shiva grants liberation (*moksha*) at the moment of death. Cremation at Manikarnika, on the banks of the Ganga, is believed to free the soul from the cycle of rebirth. That belief is why families travel great distances, and why the cremation grounds are seldom still.

Next to Manikarnika is **Harishchandra Ghat**, the city's other cremation ghat (`/places/ghats/harishchandra`).

### Tradition & belief
- **The name:** according to legend, an earring (*manikarnika*) belonging to the goddess Parvati, or by some accounts to Shiva, fell into a sacred pool here. The **Manikarnika Kund**, a stepped tank at the ghat, is associated with this story.
- According to tradition, the sacred fire here has burned continuously for a very long time, and families take fire from it to light the pyres. Accounts of this tradition vary.
- The **Doms**, a community who have traditionally tended the cremation grounds for generations, play a central role in the rites.

## Architecture and what to notice

If you pass Manikarnika on a boat or on the riverfront path, you may notice the following. Please observe them from a respectful distance.
- **The Manikarnika Kund**, the sacred stepped pool associated with the ghat's name.
- **Older temples and buildings** around the ghat, some leaning or partly submerged because of shifts in the riverbank over time. One of these is the leaning **Ratneshwar Mahadev Temple**, which stands between Manikarnika and Scindia Ghat. [VERIFY: name and location against a published source]
- **Stacks of firewood** along the ghat. They show how central this work is to the life of the city.

## How to visit respectfully

These are the most important points on this page.

1. **Do not photograph or film cremations, mourners or bodies.** Not from the ghat, not from a boat, not with a long lens. Keep your phone away. Cremation is a private family ritual, not a spectacle.
2. **Keep your distance.** Stay well away from the pyres and the paths used by funeral processions. If a procession passes, step aside quietly.
3. **Be quiet and modest.** Keep your voice low and dress modestly. Don't laugh, eat or point.
4. **Don't treat it as an attraction.** Avoid lingering. A few quiet minutes from a distance, or passing by on a boat, is enough to understand the place.
5. **Follow local instructions** from anyone working at the ghat.

### A note on requests for money
It is **commonly reported** that people at or near Manikarnika approach visitors to "explain" the rites and then ask for a donation, often for wood for the poor or the elderly. Some may say that photography is allowed in return for payment. These approaches are not official. You are under no obligation to engage or to pay. If you would like to give to charity, do so through a registered organisation you have chosen yourself.

## Photography etiquette

- **No photography of cremations, mourners or bodies, ever.**
- Even general photos of the ghat can capture grieving families. If you are on a boat and want a photo of the riverfront, wait until you are well past the cremation areas.
- On our site, we only show distant images of the ghat's architecture, with no cremations or identifiable people. We ask our partners to follow the same rule.

## Accessibility and senior-friendly guidance

- The surrounding lanes are narrow, uneven and busy with processions. For visitors with limited mobility, **viewing from a boat** is the gentlest and most respectful option. (Suggested)
- Smoke and heat from the pyres can affect people with breathing difficulties. Keep your distance.

## Getting there and last-mile walking

- **By river:** most boat rides between Assi and Dashashwamedh continue north past Manikarnika. Your boatman can stop at a respectful distance or keep moving.
- **On foot:** Manikarnika is reached through the narrow lanes of the old city, north of Dashashwamedh, or along the riverfront path when the water is low. Funeral processions use the same lanes. Give way to them. [VERIFY: current access routes]

## Frequently asked questions

**Can tourists visit Manikarnika Ghat?**
The riverfront is public, and many visitors pass by on foot or by boat. Please do so quietly and from a distance. It is an active cremation ground and a place of mourning.

**Can I take photos at Manikarnika Ghat?**
Please don't photograph or film cremations, mourners or bodies. That applies even if someone at the ghat says it is allowed in exchange for money.

**Do I need to pay anything?**
No. Visitors frequently report being asked for donations, but these requests are not official, and you don't need to pay anything to pass by.

**Why are cremations performed at Manikarnika?**
Many Hindus believe that cremation in Kashi, on the Ganga, grants liberation (*moksha*). Families travel from all over India for the last rites here.

**Is it appropriate to bring children?**
That's a personal decision. If you do, prepare them beforehand and consider viewing from a boat at a distance.

**Who can explain the rituals to me respectfully?**
A verified local guide booked in advance can explain the traditions in a respectful way. Please avoid people who approach you at the ghat.
